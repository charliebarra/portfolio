Design Notebook

Short thoughts on games, rules, and why players do weird things.

These are short observations, not finished essays. I wrote them down before the details disappeared.

Why randomness can help strategy

In Meteor Mayhem, I wanted hazards to change the situation without choosing the winner for the players. I am still testing where exciting uncertainty turns into frustration.

When one strategy starts taking over

During Meteor Mayhem testing, rushing the mining tier started to look like the preferred approach. That did not make the game broken, but it gave me a specific question for the next playtest.

When the level has to teach

In Swap Monsters, I used signs for the controls, but I also placed gaps, drops, and crystal barriers where each character's ability made sense. I would still want to watch a new player try it without help.

Why the rough version mattered

The first Meteor Mayhem prototype was not polished. It was useful because people could finally play it and show me what I had misunderstood.

Charlie Barra
